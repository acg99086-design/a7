The Thermoregulating Protein Structure of Pure Mulberry Silk Fibers

Deconstructing fibroin amino acid sequences, natural sericin moisture barrier retention, thermal breathability, and soothing sensitive skin.

The human skin is the body's largest organ, continuously regulating temperature, releasing moisture vapor, and interacting with surrounding environmental textiles. When we sleep or lounge in synthetic petroleum fabrics (such as polyester satin or nylon), non-breathable fibers trap heat and sweat against the skin, fostering bacterial growth and disrupting restful sleep. Plant fibers like cotton, while breathable, absorb vital epidermal moisture and natural oils, leaving skin dry and irritated.

Pure Mulberry silk operates on a completely different biological principle: it is not a plant carbohydrate or synthetic plastic, but a Natural Animal Protein Fiber composed of eighteen essential amino acids. At Gloss Robe Peak, our dressing gowns harness the dermatological and thermoregulating genius of Silk Fibroin and Sericin Proteins. In this masterclass, our textile biochemists and dermatological researchers explore the molecular biology of silk and its transformative benefits for skin health.

1. The Molecular Anatomy of Silk: Fibroin and Sericin Proteins

A raw silk filament produced by the Bombyx mori silkworm consists of two primary proteins:

  • The Fibroin Structural Core (75%–80%): Two twin protein filaments (brins) composed of repetitive amino acid sequences—primarily glycine, alanine, and serine—arranged in tight antiparallel beta-pleated sheets, providing immense tensile strength.
  • The Sericin Gum Envelope (20%–25%): A hydrophilic globular protein coating that binds fibroin filaments together. Sericin is rich in natural moisturizing factors (NMF) that closely mimic human epidermal proteins.
  • Degumming and Luster Activation: During artisan processing, silk is gently degummed in soft mountain water to remove excess sericin while preserving essential amino acids on the fiber surface.

“Silk does not merely touch the skin; it harmonizes with it. Its eighteen amino acids create a soothing, breathable cocoon that respects your natural skin barrier.”

2. Dynamic Thermoregulation: Cool in Summer, Warm in Winter

How silk maintains optimal body temperature across shifting ambient seasons:

  1. Hollow Micro-Voids in the Fiber Core: Microscopic pores between fibroin filaments trap motionless air, providing natural thermal insulation in cool winter boudoirs.
  2. Capillary Moisture Evaporation in Heat: In warm summer weather, the hydrophilic amino acids absorb perspiration vapor rapidly and channel it outward into the air, creating a natural evaporative cooling effect.
  3. Thermal Equilibrium Maintenance: Silk continuously equalizes temperature between your body and ambient air, preventing the midnight hot flashes and chills caused by synthetic sleepwear.
